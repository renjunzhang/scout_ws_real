\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evaluation tests whether propagated liquid dynamics change the online motion plan and reduce the measured free-surface response relative to matched slosh-agnostic controllers. Liquid performance is assessed together with completion time, path tracking, controller success, and solve time. Claims are restricted to comparisons with the matched Baseline and Smooth-only \MPCC{} variants; no superiority over existing global, obstacle-oriented, or liquid-transport planners is claimed.

\subsection{Research Questions and Primary Outcomes}

Four research questions organize the main evidence:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1---Liquid mechanism:} Does \SMPCC{} reduce the measured liquid response relative to Baseline and Smooth-only \MPCC{}?
  \item \textbf{RQ2---Time confound:} Does the benefit remain when Smooth-only \MPCC{} is matched to the completion time of \SMPCC{}?
  \item \textbf{RQ3---Container transfer:} Does geometry-based model reparameterization preserve the benefit without controller-weight retuning?
  \item \textbf{RQ4---Online planning:} Do liquid modal state and phase change the predicted motion plan, and can that plan be solved within the online deadline?
\end{itemize}

The primary outcome is the trial-level p95 of $H_{\mathrm{vis}}$ over the 10\%--90\% path-progress interval. This window is fixed before formal acquisition and is not changed after inspecting controller outcomes. The p95 over the complete 0\%--100\% motion interval is a pre-specified sensitivity outcome, and RGB RMS over a fixed $T_{\mathrm{post}}=5\,\mathrm{s}$ post-arrival window quantifies residual response. The 10\%--90\% window and $T_{\mathrm{post}}$ are pre-specified experimental-protocol parameters rather than controller implementation settings. Completion time and path-error p95 quantify performance tradeoffs. Success and solver failure are feasibility outcomes; solve-time p95 and deadline-miss rate are the real-time outcomes. One complete trial is the statistical sample. Video frames are not treated as independent observations.

Only two liquid quantities are used:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $H_{\mathrm{modal}}$ is the internal modal prediction and OCP penalty;
  \item $H_{\mathrm{vis}}$ is the calibrated vision-based experimental reference measurement and the primary physical evidence.
\end{itemize}
A fixed RGB camera provides a calibrated visual free-surface envelope, denoted by $H_{\mathrm{vis}}$. The same camera geometry, region of interest, calibration, and extraction settings are used for all controller variants. $H_{\mathrm{vis}}$ is treated as the experimental reference for physical free-surface response, whereas $H_{\mathrm{modal}}$ remains an internal model prediction. The formal configuration sets the parabolic correction off and publishes a modal-only log. Archived modal metrics are recomputed offline from $\eta_x$ and $\eta_y$ before they are used.

\subsection{Apparatus, Paths, and RGB Measurement}

Physical trials use a Scout Mini mobile base and cylindrical liquid containers
mounted at the base rotation center. The nominal container $C_1$ has inner
radius $R_c=18.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ and liquid depth $h=58\,\mathrm{mm}$; $C_2$
changes the container geometry and/or fill depth for the transfer study. A
low-risk prescribed path and a high-risk path with sustained curvature changes
are used according to the selected evidence package. Complete apparatus, path,
container, calibration, executed-command, deployment-layer, and software
metadata are reported in the supplementary material.

All controller weights, the configured damping ratio $\zeta$, executed-command
limits, shared deployment behavior, camera settings, and synchronization rules
are frozen and archived before formal acquisition. Formal RGB outcomes are not
inspected while these settings are selected.

The camera is rigidly mounted relative to the container. Its model, resolution, frame rate, pose, and region of interest are recorded. Pixel-to-millimeter calibration is performed before the formal blocks using a fixed procedure, and its resolution or repeated-calibration error is reported. The liquid-surface extraction algorithm, filtering, and missing-frame handling are specified before outcomes are inspected. The same procedure is applied to every method without using controller labels to select trials, frames, or processing parameters. RGB, odometry, executed commands, modal states, and solver records are aligned to a common timeline. The formal protocol requires each trial to begin only after the base is stationary and $H_{\mathrm{vis}}$ remains below a calibration-derived rest threshold for at least $2\,\mathrm{s}$. The threshold is determined from stationary calibration data before controller trials and is not retuned by method. Each trial includes the pre-specified $T_{\mathrm{post}}=5\,\mathrm{s}$ post-arrival observation interval. These durations are acquisition-protocol choices and do not describe internal controller timing.

Exclusions are restricted to acquisition failures unrelated to a compared method, such as a recording system that never started, an irrecoverably corrupted camera stream, or a missing path before trial onset. Such a trial is rerun within the same randomized block and both the exclusion and replacement are logged. A solver failure, timeout, path-tracking failure, or safety-triggered termination during an otherwise valid trial is retained as a method failure and is never replaced. Success is reported over all attempted valid trials. Continuous liquid and tracking outcomes are summarized for completed trials with the corresponding numerator and denominator shown; an undefined metric from a failed trial is not imputed or silently discarded. A method with a lower success rate cannot support an unconditional superiority claim based only on its completed trials. Performance values are not used to select trials or representative time series.

\subsection{Randomized Collection and Paired Analysis}

Before the first formal block, one evidence package is selected and archived:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The \emph{minimum mechanism package} contains the three methods on the high-risk path with $C_1$ and the Smooth-match--\SMPCC{} comparison, giving $5n$ trials.
  \item The \emph{two-path package} adds the three methods on the low-risk path with $C_1$, giving $8n$ trials in total.
  \item The \emph{full main package} additionally crosses $C_1$ and $C_2$ with the three methods on the high-risk path, giving $11n$ trials in total. In this package, the high-risk $C_1$ and $C_2$ trials are acquired within the same super-blocks.
\end{itemize}
Method order is randomized within each block or super-block. At $n=8$, the three packages contain 40, 64, and 88 physical trials, respectively; the optional model-mismatch group adds eight trials. The package is not expanded after inspecting formal outcomes. If the minimum package is selected, RQ1 is restricted to the high-risk path and RQ3 and all cross-container claims are removed. If the two-path package is selected, the paper may claim consistency across the two prescribed paths but still removes RQ3. The current four-RQ manuscript structure assumes the full main package.

The primary analysis uses within-block differences. For method $m$, reference method $m'$, block $b$, and path $p$,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{b,p}^{m-m'}
  =Y_{m,b,p}-Y_{m',b,p}.
  \label{eq:block_difference}
\end{equation}
The primary estimand is the mean paired difference
\[
  \bar\Delta_p^{m-m'}
  =\frac{1}{n_p}\sum_{b=1}^{n_p}\Delta_{b,p}^{m-m'},
\]
where a negative value favors \SMPCC{} when $m=\mathrm{S\text{-}MPCC}$. The median paired difference is reported only as a robustness summary and never replaces the primary estimand after outcomes are inspected. The paper reports raw trial points, block-paired lines, $\bar\Delta_p^{m-m'}$, and the mean within-block relative change. Whole blocks are resampled to form a 95\% bootstrap confidence interval when at least eight complete blocks are available. The formal target is $n=8$, but reaching eight blocks does not by itself make an effect statistically confirmed; interpretation remains based on the paired observations, effect magnitude, uncertainty interval, and failure pattern. If acquisition stops at the minimum $n=5$, the study is labeled exploratory and emphasizes raw paired observations and effect sizes without confirmatory $p$-value claims. Mixed-effects models are supporting analyses; because the low- and high-risk collections use different blocks, their random intercept is nested by path, $u_{b(p)}$.

\subsection{RQ2: Completion-Time-Matched Comparison}

Smooth-match is tuned using an independent pilot set. Only completion time is inspected during pilot tuning; RGB outcomes are not used. Its nominal speed reference is frozen when
\begin{equation}
  \frac{
  |\widetilde T_{\mathrm{smooth,match}}
  -\widetilde T_{\mathrm{S\text{-}MPCC}}|}
  {\widetilde T_{\mathrm{S\text{-}MPCC}}}
  \leq\delta_T.
  \label{eq:time_matching}
\end{equation}
Formal paired blocks are collected only after $\delta_T$ and the Smooth-match reference have been fixed. The RQ2 result figure places completion time on the horizontal axis and trial-level RGB p95 on the vertical axis, with block identity retained. Formal data are not reused to iteratively adjust the speed reference.

\subsection{RQ3: Container Transfer}

The nominal container is denoted by $C_1$. Container $C_2$ is selected so that the natural frequency from \cref{eq:modal_parameters} changes by approximately 15--20\% or more. Both containers are mounted at the base rotation center. The same liquid type, damping ratio $\zeta$, paths, reference speeds, motion constraints, horizon, solver, and controller weights are used.

The modal height reference uses a common freeboard fraction,
\[
  H_{\mathrm{ref},c}
  =\lambda_H h_{\mathrm{freeboard},c},
  \qquad c\in\{C_1,C_2\},
\]
where $\lambda_H$ is frozen before collection. Container transfer updates only
\[
  \omega_{1,c},\qquad c_{h,c},\qquad
  \eta_{\mathrm{ref},c},\qquad
  \dot\eta_{\mathrm{ref},c}.
\]
Results are reported as both $H_{\mathrm{vis}}\,[\mathrm{mm}]$ and
$H_{\mathrm{vis}}/h_{\mathrm{freeboard}}$. Method comparisons are made within a container before interpreting the method-by-container interaction.

\subsection{RQ4: State-Dependent Online Planning}

RQ4 is a controlled computational mechanism study rather than a substitute for physical liquid-response evidence. It holds the measured robot state and reference path fixed and initializes the OCP with four modal states of equal energy and different phase. The pre-specified mechanism-study protocol uses $A=0.5\eta_{\mathrm{ref}}$; this amplitude is an analysis choice rather than a controller implementation parameter:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  &[A,0,0,0]^\top,\qquad
  [0,\omega_1A,0,0]^\top,\\
  &[-A,0,0,0]^\top,\qquad
  [0,-\omega_1A,0,0]^\top.
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:phase_initial_states}
\end{equation}
For each initialization, the predicted $v_{k\mid j}$, $\omega_{k\mid j}$, $v_{s,k\mid j}$, $H_{\mathrm{modal},k\mid j}$, and first control are recorded. A multi-panel figure tests the method-level claim that the same robot state and path can yield different future plans when liquid phase changes.

Online computation is evaluated over every formal physical trial. The reported statistics are solve-time median, p95, and maximum, together with
\[
  r_{\mathrm{miss}}
  =\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{j=1}^{N_c}
  \mathbb I(T_{\mathrm{solve},j}>\Delta t).
\]
Solver failure, fallback action, and achieved control frequency are reported separately. The runtime figure uses an empirical cumulative distribution function to expose tail latency.

\subsection{Supporting Analyses}

Model consistency is diagnostic rather than a primary method outcome. With a small denominator safeguard $\epsilon_H>0$, the vision-normalized signed integral bias is
\begin{equation}
  \gamma_{\mathrm{bias}}
  =100\,
  \frac{\int_0^{T_e}(H_{\mathrm{modal}}-H_{\mathrm{vis}})\,\mathrm dt}
  {\max\{\int_0^{T_e}H_{\mathrm{vis}}\,\mathrm dt,\epsilon_H\}}.
  \label{eq:model_bias}
\end{equation}
The absolute-disagreement index replaces the numerator by
$\int_0^{T_e}|H_{\mathrm{modal}}-H_{\mathrm{vis}}|\,\mathrm dt$. These quantities evaluate the internal model against the experimental reference and cannot replace the RGB outcomes in RQ1--RQ3. Vision-based external observation and integral model--experiment comparisons have precedent in anti-slosh validation \cite{C01_Ferrari2026}, but that precedent does not establish the accuracy of the present camera, calibration, or extraction pipeline.

General sensitivity to $\omega_1$, $\zeta$, $c_h$, initial modal state, and execution delay is reported in supplementary material. The analysis presents degradation curves rather than a binary robustness claim.
