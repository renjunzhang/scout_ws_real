\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section defines the evidence required to evaluate the target method in \cref{sec:method}.
It is a preregistration-oriented protocol, not a report of completed physical results.
The current development revision has software branch evidence but has not passed all release gates; numerical performance fields therefore remain explicitly pending.

\subsection{Research Questions and Scope}

The evaluation follows the dependency order in \cref{tab:research_questions}.
Execution and total-lead validity are checked before physical control efficacy, and independent RGB measurement---not the internal slosh model---determines the physical result.

All confirmatory trials track a path frozen before motion in a static, obstacle-clear workspace.
The core path, denoted P-core, is selected for repeatable high excitation and is used for every principal comparison.
An optional P-MBF path may be generated by MBF before a trial, then frozen and assigned a separate artifact; it is considered only after the P-core claims pass.
No trial invokes online obstacle detection or replanning.
A newly observed obstacle causes the operator or an independent safety system to terminate the trial and request zero velocity.

\subsection{Platform, Frozen Assets, and Protocol}

\subsubsection{Platform and measurements}

The platform comprises the Scout mobile base, the contracted open container and fill condition, the final command-publication interface, and the localization and measurement chain.
Each trial records at least:
\begin{itemize}
  \item solver, supervisor, limiter, safety, and final published commands with source and publication timestamps;
  \item base feedback, odometry, processed IMU, and Nokov or equivalent source-stamped pose;
  \item independently calibrated RealSense RGB liquid-height signals and per-frame validity;
  \item internal liquid state, selected artifact phase, execution-front state, terminal gate metrics, execution compatibility, and solver status.
\end{itemize}

The RGB chain is the physical reference.
The internal low-order model and external online slosh monitor are secondary mechanism measurements and are reported even when they disagree with RGB.
Neither internal signal can replace missing or invalid RGB evidence.

\subsubsection{Release freeze and data separation}

Before confirmation, one release manifest freezes the Git commit and build, map and transforms, footprint, path point sequence and SHA-256, execution and liquid models, container and fill range, RGB calibration and region of interest, \OfflineSloshOCP{} artifact and contract hash, condition parameters, primary time window, stopping rules, and randomized block order.
Changing a contracted item creates a new release and invalidates reuse of the old phase objects.

The following pools are separated by complete trial or complete block, never by adjacent video frames or controller samples:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  &D_{\mathrm{id,fit}},\ D_{\mathrm{id,test}},\quad
  D_{\mathrm{gate,fit}},\ D_{\mathrm{gate,test}},\\
  &D_{\mathrm{dev,tune}},\ D_{\mathrm{dev,pilot}},\quad
  D_{\mathrm{main,confirm}},\ D_{\mathrm{recovery,confirm}}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:data_pools}
\end{equation}
Identification test data do not select the model, gate test data do not tune radii or thresholds, and a pilot followed by any retuning is replaced by a new pilot.
The two confirmation pools are collected only after the release is frozen.

One complete trial is the statistical unit.
Controller cycles, path samples, and video frames are repeated measurements within that trial.
Method failures, zero requests, timeouts, solver failures, and safety interventions remain in the denominator.
Only a preregistered external acquisition fault may trigger a replacement, and the original attempt remains in the audit log.

\paragraph{G0: execution and total lead.}
Linear and angular delay, actuator time constant, gain, and applicability range are identified separately from final $u^{\mathrm{pub}}$.
Pure-delay, first-order, and any richer candidate are compared on $D_{\mathrm{id,test}}$.
The evaluation aligns command, Nokov/pose, odometry, IMU, and RGB source times and reports error through the full lead in \eqref{eq:total_lead}, not only the last $N_\ell\Delta t$.
It also verifies that the candidate first action has detectable influence at the joint terminal and audits processed-IMU magnitude and phase near the approximately $4.97$-Hz liquid mode.

\paragraph{G1: offline artifact.}
P-core receives a complete formal artifact rather than a development metadata object.
State and command constraints, terminal ownership, nonphysical spikes, and tail truncation are checked directly.
Parameter-perturbed and higher-fidelity simulations compare A0, and actual-input replay separates execution mismatch from liquid-model mismatch.

\paragraph{G2--G4: measurement, gate, and pilot.}
RGB validity and detectability are frozen before looking at controller effects.
The empirical gate is fit only on $D_{\mathrm{gate,fit}}$ and evaluated on $D_{\mathrm{gate,test}}$.
C1/C4 tuning ends before $D_{\mathrm{dev,pilot}}$; that pilot decides GO/NO-GO and never enters the final performance estimate.
No failed gate may be bypassed by widening the empirical set, enlarging residual limits, admitting a development artifact, excluding failed trials, or increasing the formal sample size.

\subsection{Main Physical Evaluation}

\subsubsection{Primary outcome and task constraints}

The sole primary physical outcome is the trial-level 95th percentile of the independently extracted RGB max-LCR signal over a preregistered motion-plus-tail window,
\begin{equation}
  Y_{\mathrm{RGB}}
  =\operatorname{P95}_{\,t\in\mathcal W_{\mathrm{motion+tail}}}
  \left(
    \operatorname{max\text{-}LCR}_{\mathrm{RGB}}(t)
  \right).
  \label{eq:primary_rgb_outcome}
\end{equation}
Motion onset, arrival, tail duration, invalid-frame handling, physical units, and left/center/right composition are frozen before confirmation.
Peak, RMS, tail RMS, and settling time are secondary; the analysis cannot select whichever summary is most favorable after acquisition.

Task and fairness outcomes include completion time; contour, yaw, and endpoint errors; success and timeout; zero requests; safety events; and the distributions of $v$, $\omega$, acceleration, angular acceleration, jerk, and command spectrum.
Internal liquid peak/RMS/phase, residual magnitude, saturation, selected-phase jumps, gate coverage, and rejoining time are mechanism outcomes only.

\subsubsection{Paired estimands and decision rule}

Within each randomized block $b$, the primary paired effect is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_b^{\mathrm{C4-C0}}=Y_{b,\mathrm{C4}}-Y_{b,\mathrm{C0}},
  \label{eq:primary_paired_effect}
\end{equation}
where lower is better.
The trial-level paired effects, a robust aggregate, an uncertainty interval, raw points, failure denominator, and leave-one-block-out sensitivity are reported together.
Sample size is determined from a preregistered smallest effect of interest (SESOI), RGB minimum detectable difference, and variance estimated from a pilot independent of confirmation; no historical fixed-count matrix is reused.

The claim ``improves anti-slosh performance relative to C0 OrdinaryMPCC under the frozen conditions'' is unlocked only if C4 versus C0 reaches the RGB SESOI, completion time and tracking satisfy preregistered non-inferiority margins, and failures and safety events do not worsen beyond their margins.
Only after that test passes is the analogous C4 versus C1 contrast interpreted confirmatorily under fixed-sequence gatekeeping.
If RGB improves only by becoming materially slower, less accurate, or less reliable, the result is reported as a tradeoff rather than superiority.

The main nominal matrix contains C0, C1, C2, and C4 on P-core.
C3 is evaluated with C4 under controlled deviations in the recovery study.
Formal numerical results remain \Pending{} until G0--G4 pass and $D_{\mathrm{main,confirm}}$ is collected under one frozen release.

\subsection{Component Ablations}

A0 tests whether the offline liquid objective adds value beyond a kinematically smooth replay under the same grid and execution model.
A1 then tests whether a finite residual improves tracking or physical liquid response relative to phase-aligned zero residual.
A2 evaluates the recovery mechanism jointly; it cannot attribute an effect separately to the 9-D gate, execution compatibility set, or stored action.
A3 first compares prediction on held-out simulation or replay and, if an efficacy statement is desired, uses an independently frozen paired control experiment.

For each pair, the paper reports both the intended change and a manipulation check: offline liquid cost and tail, residual norm, gate branch, buffer prediction, or published-command separation, as applicable.
An ablation that does not produce a detectable change at the final publication interface cannot support a physical causal interpretation.
No component claim is inferred from the ordering of C0--C4.

\subsection{Recovery-Gate Evaluation}

Recovery success is labeled independently of the internal gate.
In simulation, the label uses a held-out higher-fidelity outcome, true constraints, compatible execution state, and the complete tail.
On hardware, it uses independent RGB, task and safety constraints, and a preregistered tail-settling rule.
Evaluation of the 9-D classifier is restricted to samples inside $\mathcal B_i^{\mathrm{exec}}$; incompatible samples are rejected by contract and their rejection rate is reported separately.

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Held-out empirical-gate outcomes.}
  \label{tab:gate_confusion}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
    \toprule
    Gate decision & Recovery succeeds & Recovery fails \\
    \midrule
    Accept & True accept & \textbf{False accept} \\
    Reject & False reject & True reject \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The primary gate diagnostic is the conditional false-safe fraction
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{FS}|\mathrm{A}}
  =\frac{N(\mathrm{gate\ accept}\ \cap\ \mathrm{recovery\ fail})}
  {N(\mathrm{gate\ accept})},
  \label{eq:conditional_false_safe}
\end{equation}
an estimate of $P(\mathrm{recovery\ fail}\mid\mathrm{gate\ accept})$ rather than the conventional false-positive rate.
It is undefined when no sample is accepted; that case is reported together with zero coverage rather than assigned a favorable value.
Otherwise it is reported with an uncertainty interval, not hidden inside overall accuracy.
The paper also reports false rejects, normal-operation and per-phase coverage, the worst phase, rejoining time, and gate-outside zero requests.
For the phase-indexed stored action $u_{\mathrm{rec}}(i)$, proposal, passage through the limiter/safety chain, and actual publication are separate rates.
Fit rollouts never reappear in this table.

The recovery control matrix has two parts: M4a evaluates the frozen gate on $D_{\mathrm{gate,test}}$, while M4b compares C3 and C4 on $D_{\mathrm{recovery,confirm}}$ under small preregistered pose offsets, added command delay/limits, or a repeatable short speed gate.
Large unsafe deviations remain in simulation or replay.
Unrepeatable manual pushing and dynamic obstacles are not formal disturbances.

\subsection{Runtime, Command Integrity, and Limitations}

Runtime reporting includes solve-time p50/p95/maximum, the fraction exceeding $33.3$ ms, achieved publication frequency, solver failure, stale state, contract invalidation, supervisor branch, residual saturation, limiter rewrite, and safety override.
The audit follows each proposed first action through \eqref{eq:publication_chain} and verifies that only the final $u^{\mathrm{pub}}$ updates the two delay buffers.
Development branch coverage or no-motion tests establish wiring only; they do not establish physical efficacy.

The final interpretation is bounded by the frozen path, static obstacle-clear workspace, contracted container/fill and low-order liquid-model range, identified Scout execution dynamics, and empirical gate coverage.
The stored action is not a feedback recovery controller, and no recursive-feasibility, robust-recovery, or spill-free guarantee is made.
At the present revision, B0, the formal offline artifact, execution compatibility, held-out gate, and C2/C3/C4 physical runners remain unresolved; physical \texttt{enforce} is therefore \NoGo{} and no positive anti-slosh result is claimed.
