\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evaluation is organized around a prescribed-path trajectory-generation
claim: whether an odometry-driven, model-propagated internal liquid modal state changes
finite-horizon virtual progress and chassis motion, induces nontrivial local
motion-timing redistribution, and improves the measured free-surface response at
comparable task efficiency. Trajectory changes are mechanism evidence, whereas
$H_{\mathrm{vis}}$ is physical-effect evidence; neither substitutes for the
other. Formal outcomes are not reported in this draft because the comparator,
method-release, measurement, and analysis gates have not yet produced an
immutable acquisition release.

\subsection{Evaluation Questions and Evidence Structure}

Four research questions order the evidence:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1---Physical effectiveness and trajectory redistribution:}
  On the reported high-risk path, does \SMPCC{} reduce the calibrated visual
  response relative to Baseline and Smooth-only \MPCC{}, preserve task
  performance, and redistribute executed timing and longitudinal/lateral
  excitation in path-feature-dependent segments?
  \item \textbf{RQ2---Competing timing explanations:} Does the benefit remain
  against completion-time-matched Smooth-only \MPCC{} and against a
  model-informed fixed-profile condition that uses the same nominal liquid
  parameters but no runtime model-propagated internal modal-state memory?
  \item \textbf{RQ3---Model-state-dependent online generation and execution:}
  Do constructed internal modal phases and the model state supplied to the
  online solver change the complete predicted plan and optimized first action;
  do formal \SMPCC{} plan differences survive the shared execution layer, and
  is the OCP solved within the online budget?
  \item \textbf{RQ4---Path-feature selectivity and limited configuration
  transfer:} Does the frozen method avoid unnecessary conservatism on the
  reported low-risk path, and, only if the preregistered second-container stage
  is executed, does it satisfy the declared physical--task transfer criteria?
\end{itemize}

Let $\bm c=[v,\omega]^\top$ denote a base velocity command. The evidence chain is
\[
\begin{aligned}
(\kappa,\hat{\bm x}_{r,j},\hat{\bm x}_{\ell,j})
&\rightarrow
\{s_{\mathrm{ocp},0:N\mid j}^{\star},
  \bm u_{0:N-1\mid j}^{\star}\}\\
&\rightarrow \bm c_{\mathrm{solver},j}
\rightarrow \bm c_{\mathrm{post},j}
\rightarrow \bm c_{\mathrm{pub},j}\\
&\rightarrow s_{\mathrm{proj}}(t)
\rightarrow \{a_{x,\mathrm{exec}},a_{y,\mathrm{exec}}\}\\
&\Rightarrow \{H_{\mathrm{modal}},H_{\mathrm{vis}}\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The final branch denotes parallel model and physical responses, not a claim that
$H_{\mathrm{modal}}$ causes $H_{\mathrm{vis}}$. Fixed-profile reference timing,
\SMPCC{} predicted timing, and odometry-derived executed timing are retained as
separate layers. Only published commands and measured motion connect directly
to realized physical excitation. A replay that stops before the shared
execution layer is interpreted only as an optimized action or timing
difference.

\subsection{Experimental Setup, Compared Conditions, and Matrix}

\subsubsection{Platform, paths, containers, and trial admission}

Physical trials use a Scout Mini mobile base with a cylindrical liquid container
mounted at the base rotation center and a fixed RGB measurement camera. The
body-fixed $x_b$ axis points forward and $y_b$ points laterally. The nominal
container is denoted by $C_1$. A high-risk path $H_1$ contains uninterrupted
start-up, sustained curvature, curvature reversal, exit re-acceleration, and
terminal braking so that the propagated internal model state can influence later
path segments. A
low-risk path $L_1$ preserves the same task interface but reduces curvature and
excitation; it is a selectivity condition rather than a strict statistical
negative control. Path hashes, lengths, curvature summaries, direction, and
$Z_1$--$Z_5$ mechanism-segment boundaries are frozen before formal outcomes.

Container $C_2$ is introduced in the manuscript only if the conditional transfer
stage is preregistered and completed. Its geometry, liquid/freeboard convention,
model parameters, visual-measurement admission, and analysis rule are fixed
before the first formal trial; it is never chosen after inspecting Stage~I or
Stage~II-A outcomes.

Every trial must independently satisfy the same entry rule: initial position and
heading, zero command, a ring-down-validated minimum settling time
$T_{\mathrm{settle}}$, a method-independent monitor when available, successful
reset of applicable method state and the shared monitor, and a maximum waiting
time. If no online physical-liquid monitor is available, the pre-motion rule
uses the frozen $T_{\mathrm{settle}}$. A timestamp-audited RGB window of at
least $2\,\mathrm{s}$ precedes first effective motion, but it does not by itself
establish rest. A frozen, condition-blinded offline visual-start quality check
subsequently determines measurement eligibility.
Timeout, failed settling, acquisition failure, and split-block handling are
preregistered so that one condition cannot contaminate the next through residual
motion.

Baseline, Smooth-only, and \SMPCC{} are the three internal variants of the common
OCP in \cref{sec:method}. Smooth-match is a separately frozen speed-reference
configuration of Smooth-only and is not an additional algorithmic contribution.
The fixed-profile condition is a separate timing-generation/tracking system, so
its comparison with \SMPCC{} is an end-to-end system comparison rather than a
single-factor \emph{memory} ablation.

The default fixed-profile proposal is a Hamaguchi-inspired two-impulse ZV
fixed-path timing condition, grounded in classical input shaping and mobile-base
liquid-transfer work \cite{A07_Singer1990,B03_Hamaguchi2004,B02_Hamaguchi2005}.
It uses the same frozen geometry, $\omega_1$, $\zeta$, and motion limits as
\SMPCC{}, and explicitly accounts for longitudinal excitation and
$a_y\simeq v^2\kappa=v\omega$ on the supplied path. The impulse amplitudes and
delay $\Delta T$ determined from $\omega_1$ and $\zeta$ remain fixed. Completion
time is tuned only through one preregistered unshaped base-profile parameter;
each candidate is regenerated, shaped, integrated, and checked against the
constraints. A completed shaped profile is never time-warped. The profile is
generated from the same zero/near-zero nominal entry assumption used by the
physical rest gate and is not replanned from a runtime model-propagated internal
modal state. Tracker error,
latency, command correction, offline generation time, and online tracking cost
are reported. Unless a separate fidelity audit is completed, this condition is
called prior-art-inspired and not a reproduction of the cited methods.

\subsubsection{Pre-acquisition gates and staged matrix}

The exact development protocol is archived outside the page-limited main text,
but formal acquisition cannot begin until the following gates pass:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the bounded prescribed-path claim and all five conditions are fixed;
  \item current and rotation-consistent liquid-dynamics candidates pass the
  preregistered rotation-relevance decision; distinguishable differences trigger
  a rotation-only RGB pilot before one method release is selected;
  \item a small candidate set of weights is screened for safety, local timing,
  tracking, and runtime without treating the internal modal response as physical
  truth;
  \item one final candidate passes an independent RGB efficacy pilot against
  Smooth-only using an exact preregistered development block count (default
  proposal $n_{\mathrm{dev}}=4$) and no early stopping;
  \item the odometry-based trajectory extractor and immutable phase/replay tools
  pass reproduction smoke tests;
  \item Smooth-match and fixed-profile tuning, common constraints, tracking
  behavior, and comparable development budgets are frozen; and
  \item measurement, failure, randomization, estimand, and software/configuration
  hashes are bound to one read-only release identifier.
\end{enumerate}
Changing a candidate, weight, comparator, or gate creates a new development
release and a new independent efficacy check. Development trials are archived
and never pooled with formal inference.

Stage~I is a randomized complete block: every condition occurs once and all four
contrasts with \SMPCC{} share the same block-level \SMPCC{} observation.
Stage~II-A replaces repeated basic anchoring with the two competitive timing
conditions and tests path-feature selectivity. Stage~II-B is a conditional,
predeclared extension rather than a result-driven container choice. Because
Stage~II does not include Smooth-match, it supports a joint
liquid-response--efficiency--tracking interpretation; a strict completion-time
comparison on those configurations would require adding Smooth-match and
recomputing the matrix.

The provisional $n=8$ is a resource proposal, not an a priori guarantee of
precision. Before the first formal trial, $n$ is calculated from a minimum
meaningful visual-response effect $\delta_H$, development paired variability,
target interval width or power, and allowances for method failure and invalid
vision. Formal data are never used to add only favorable samples.

Stage~II opens only after the preregistered Stage~I extension gate passes. The
gate requires traceability of all attempts, frozen RGB/synchronization admission,
the primary Smooth-only and key Fixed-profile criteria, the Smooth-match timing
gate, success/tracking/runtime/intervention tolerances, no single-block dominance,
and operational trajectory, phase/replay, and execution-chain audits. Failure
stops extension and is reported under the frozen analysis; it is not repaired by
retuning or adding $L_1/C_2$ trials.

\subsection{Measurements, Protocol, and Analysis}

\subsubsection{Recorded signals and executed trajectory}

Each trial records synchronized signals at the level at which they enter the
evidence chain:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the frozen path/hash, curvature $\kappa(s)$, odometry-derived progress
  $s_{\mathrm{proj}}$, and, for online \MPCC{} conditions, OCP virtual progress
  $s_{\mathrm{ocp}}$;
  \item odometry $(x,y,\theta,v,\omega)$ and independently reconstructed
  cross-track/contour error;
  \item actual executed timing $t(\sigma)$ for
  $\sigma=s_{\mathrm{proj}}/L$, $Z_1$--$Z_5$ traversal times, and fixed-profile
  reference $s_{\mathrm{fp,ref}}(t)$, $v_{\mathrm{fp,ref}}(s)$, and
  $\omega_{\mathrm{fp,ref}}(s)$;
  \item for online \MPCC{} conditions, the complete pre-solve robot state and
  immutable solver, warm-start, previous-control, path, reference, and
  deployment context; for \SMPCC{}, this also includes the model-propagated
  internal modal state;
  \item for online \MPCC{} conditions, the optimized first action and complete
  predicted $s_{\mathrm{ocp}}$, $v$, $\omega$, $v_s$, $a$, and $\alpha$
  horizon; for \SMPCC{}, the complete modal horizon is also retained;
  \item raw method-native command, post-gate command, published command, and
  limiter/fallback/intervention flags;
  \item for \SMPCC{}, model-propagated internal state
  $(\eta_x,\dot\eta_x,\eta_y,\dot\eta_y)$ and $H_{\mathrm{modal}}$;
  \item for every physical condition, calibrated $H_{\mathrm{vis}}$ and all
  vision-quality flags; and
  \item for online \MPCC{}, solve time/status and budget overrun; for
  Fixed-profile, tracking time/status; and, for every condition, fallback and
  achieved control interval.
\end{itemize}

The actual $t(\sigma)$ analysis never uses $s_{\mathrm{ocp}}$ as ground truth.
Before formal outcomes, the odometry-to-path projection, monotonic guard,
backtracking, projection-jump, stall, first-arrival, interpolation, cross-track,
and terminal rules are frozen. Because
$s_{\mathrm{ocp},0\mid j}=s_{\mathrm{proj}}(t_j)$ by initialization, their
current-node difference is identically zero and is not reported as evidence. If
retained, the forecast-consistency diagnostic is instead
\begin{equation}
  e_s(k\mid j)
  =s_{\mathrm{ocp},k\mid j}^{\star}
  -s_{\mathrm{proj}}(t_j+k\Delta t),
  \qquad k>0,
  \label{eq:progress_forecast_error}
\end{equation}
using interpolated future odometry only over valid observed horizons and frozen
horizon/segment summaries. It never replaces actual $t(\sigma)$. Predicted
$v_s$ describes optimizer intent and is not a measured base speed.

Realized excitation is derived from synchronized odometry using the frozen
filtering and differentiation procedure:
\[
  a_{x,\mathrm{exec}}\simeq\dot v_{\mathrm{odom}},
  \qquad
  a_{y,\mathrm{exec}}\simeq
  v_{\mathrm{odom}}\omega_{\mathrm{odom}}.
\]
Predicted $a$ and $\alpha$ are used only for mechanism analysis.

For frozen command tolerances $\epsilon_v$ and $\epsilon_\omega$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  r_{\mathrm{int}}
  =\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{j=1}^{N_c}
  \mathbb I\!\big(&
  |v_{\mathrm{pub},j}-v_{\mathrm{raw},j}|>\epsilon_v\\[-1mm]
  &\lor
  |\omega_{\mathrm{pub},j}-\omega_{\mathrm{raw},j}|>\epsilon_\omega
  \big).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:intervention_ratio}
\end{equation}
Here \emph{raw} means the solver command for the online \MPCC{} conditions and the
method-native tracker command for Fixed-profile. If the shared layer frequently
overrides raw differences, the physical outcome is attributed to the final
executed trajectory rather than directly to unexecuted optimizer output.

\subsubsection{Physical, task, trajectory, and runtime outcomes}

$H_{\mathrm{vis}}$ is the calibrated vision-based experimental reference, not
unconditional ground truth. $H_{\mathrm{modal}}$ is an internal nonnegative
model response and never decides the physical winner. Camera geometry, region of
interest, pixel-to-length calibration, extraction, filtering, missing-frame
handling, synchronization, and uncertainty are frozen without method labels.

The primary physical window and outcome are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal W_{\mathrm{full}}
  &=[t_{\mathrm{move}},t_{\mathrm{arrival}}],\\
  H_{\mathrm{vis,p95}}^{\mathrm{full}}
  &=Q_{0.95}\!\left(
  H_{\mathrm{vis}}(t):t\in\mathcal W_{\mathrm{full}}
  \right).
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:primary_rgb_outcome}
\end{equation}
The full window includes start-up and terminal braking. The 10\%--90\%
progress-window p95 is a key sensitivity result, not a replacement for the
primary outcome. Secondary outcomes are full-motion and post-arrival
$H_{\mathrm{vis}}$ RMS, completion time, odometry-derived contour-error p95,
and success.

The primary trajectory/mechanism quantities are actual $t(\sigma)$,
$Z_1$--$Z_5$ traversal-time vectors, preregistered braking and re-acceleration
features, residual after fitting a single constant speed scaling, $a_x$ RMS,
$|a_y|$ p95, $H_{\mathrm{modal}}$, and $r_{\mathrm{int}}$. Runtime reporting
contains solve/tracking-time median, p95, and maximum, budget-overrun rate,
solver/tracker failure, fallback, and achieved frequency. Grid points on process
curves and video frames are not statistical samples; one complete trial is the
sample.

\subsubsection{Randomization, estimands, and failure handling}

Stage~I order follows a newly generated position-balanced five-condition random
table; Stage~II uses independently randomized three-condition blocks. Blocks
from different paths, containers, or dates are not linked merely because they
share an index. The confirmatory family uses fixed sequential gatekeeping:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \SMPCC{} minus Smooth-only for the primary physical effect;
  \item only if that gate passes, \SMPCC{} minus Fixed-profile for the key
  novelty comparison.
\end{enumerate}
The Smooth-match completion-time gate and the frozen success tolerance must also
hold for the full claim. \SMPCC{} minus Baseline is secondary, and Smooth-only
minus Baseline is a mechanism diagnostic. Claims are not rewritten around the
observed significance pattern.

For a trial-level outcome $Y$, the block contrast is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_b^{m-m'}=Y_{m,b}-Y_{m',b}.
  \label{eq:block_difference}
\end{equation}
The manuscript reports raw trial points, paired lines, the mean paired
difference, relative change, whole-block bootstrap intervals, and
leave-one-block-out sensitivity. Exact randomization/permutation inference
reproduces the allowed assignments of the actual five-condition randomization.
A paired sign-flip analysis, if retained, is only a sensitivity under an
additional symmetry assumption and is not called the exact randomization test.
Smooth-match, Baseline, and other secondary comparisons follow a preregistered
hierarchy or Holm adjustment.

All valid formal attempts remain in the fixed planned-unit denominator.
Method-related solver failure, timeout, tracking failure, or safety termination
is a method failure and is never replaced by a successful rerun. Only an
acquisition failure unrelated to condition can be excluded and replaced within
the same block, with both events archived. A failed offline visual-start check is
an acquisition failure, not a retrospective method failure. Continuous outcomes
use pairs in
which both methods yield defined values, report $n_{\mathrm{pair}}/n$, and are
explicitly called success-conditional paired effects. The analysis additionally
includes a preregistered failure-penalized worst-rank or composite win--loss
sensitivity. A performance claim requires the continuous effect and success
noninferiority tolerance to hold together.
Fallback events remain in the method record; whether they imply trial-level
failure or are judged by a frozen event-rate tolerance is preregistered.

\subsection{RQ1: Physical Effectiveness and Trajectory Redistribution}

RQ1 uses only the Stage~I $C_1+H_1$ randomized complete blocks. The physical
result is reported for all five conditions, but the first primary contrast is
\SMPCC{} minus Smooth-only. Method-wise summaries are descriptive; the
interpretation uses paired effects, uncertainty, failure denominators, and
leave-one-block-out behavior.

The central process figure aligns executed curves by
$\sigma=s_{\mathrm{proj}}/L$ and shows at minimum path curvature, actual
$t(\sigma)$ or segment traversal time, odometry-derived $v_{\mathrm{odom}}$ and
$\omega_{\mathrm{odom}}$, realized
$a_x$ and $a_y$, and $H_{\mathrm{vis}}$. \SMPCC{}, Smooth-only, and
Fixed-profile occupy the main panels; complete Baseline and Smooth-match curves
can be archived outside the page-limited body. XY trajectories verify that the
same prescribed geometry was executed but are not used as the primary mechanism
argument.

Interpretation asks whether \SMPCC{} changes braking before sustained curvature
or reversal, changes entry/reversal speed, and changes re-acceleration after the
demanding segment. A mechanism-consistent timing--excitation--visual sequence is
reported cautiously and is not called a formal mediation analysis. Any
completion-time, tracking, success, or execution-intervention penalty is
reported with the physical effect.

\subsection{RQ2: Competing Timing Explanations}

Smooth-match tests uniform slowdown. Its speed reference is tuned using
independent development data and frozen when
\begin{equation}
  \frac{
  |\widetilde T_{\mathrm{smooth,match}}
  -\widetilde T_{\mathrm{S\text{-}MPCC}}|}
  {\widetilde T_{\mathrm{S\text{-}MPCC}}}
  \leq\delta_T,
  \label{eq:time_matching}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_T$ is fixed before formal acquisition. Formal trials remain in the
analysis even when realized time subsequently drifts outside the development
target. Both medians in \cref{eq:time_matching} are development-set completion
times reconstructed from $s_{\mathrm{proj}}$ under the frozen first-arrival and
terminal rules.

Fixed-profile tests whether nominal liquid-aware timing is sufficient without
runtime modal-state memory. Its generator, physical parameters, impulse
sequence, one allowed base-profile tuning parameter, regenerated profile/hash,
and tracker are frozen from development data. A single fixed operating point
does not establish a time--response Pareto frontier. If \SMPCC{} outperforms
Smooth-match but not Fixed-profile, the paper may still report model-state
sensitivity in RQ3 but cannot claim an end-to-end physical advantage over fixed
physics-aware timing. Even a favorable Fixed-profile result does not isolate the
physical causal necessity of memory without a physical NoState ablation.

The RQ2 display combines paired completion-time versus
$H_{\mathrm{vis,p95}}^{\mathrm{full}}$ observations with actual
$t(\sigma)$, $v_{\mathrm{odom}}(\sigma)$,
$a_{y,\mathrm{exec}}(\sigma)$, and
$H_{\mathrm{vis}}(\sigma)$ for Smooth-match, Fixed-profile, and \SMPCC{}.

\subsection{RQ3: Model-State-Dependent Online Generation and Execution}
\label{sec:model_state_mechanism}

\subsubsection{Constructed internal modal-phase study}

Two mechanism checkpoints are fixed before formal outcomes: one longitudinal
checkpoint near start-up or re-acceleration and one lateral checkpoint near
curve entry or curvature reversal. Robot state, path, container parameters, and
modal energy are fixed; only one internal model-phase direction changes. For a
frozen amplitude $A$, the four longitudinal states are
\[
\begin{aligned}
  &[A,0,0,0]^\top,\qquad [0,\omega_1A,0,0]^\top,\\
  &[-A,0,0,0]^\top,\qquad [0,-\omega_1A,0,0]^\top,
\end{aligned}
\]
with the analogous four states in the lateral coordinate. Each solve exports
the complete predicted $s_{\mathrm{ocp}}$, $v$, $\omega$, $v_s$, $a$,
$\alpha$, and modal horizon and the optimized first action. Differences below
the frozen numerical tolerance do not support phase-sensitive generation.
Differences above it establish sensitivity to constructed internal model phase,
not accuracy of the true signed liquid phase.

\subsubsection{Online-input/zero-state replay}

For a formal high-risk cycle $j$, an immutable pre-solve snapshot
$\mathcal S_j$ is cloned into
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{online},j}
&=\mathcal P(\hat{\bm x}_{r,j},\hat{\bm x}_{\ell,j},
\mathrm{path}_j,\mathcal S_j),\\
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{zero},j}
&=\mathcal P(\hat{\bm x}_{r,j},\bm 0,
\mathrm{path}_j,\mathcal S_j).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:online_zero_replay}
\end{equation}
Here \emph{online} means the model-propagated internal state supplied to the
online solver, not a measured physical liquid state. The online-input branch must reproduce
the online solver status, first action, and raw command within frozen
tolerances. Nonreproduced cycles are reported as replay failures.

Trial-level summaries include
$\Delta a_0$, $\Delta\alpha_0$, $\Delta v_{s,0}$, complete-horizon timing
differences, and the fraction of cycles above numerical tolerance. Control
cycles are not treated as independent samples. Unless both branches also pass
through immutable copies of the shared execution layer, these quantities are
called optimized first-action/timing differences, not counterfactual executed
commands. The replay establishes only that the propagated internal state affects
optimization; it does not establish true-phase accuracy or a physical benefit of
the zero-state branch.

\subsubsection{Execution preservation and real-time feasibility}

Formal logs report raw, post-gate, published, and odometry-level difference
preservation, $r_{\mathrm{int}}$, and all runtime failures. For online
conditions, the budget-overrun rate is
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{miss}}
  =\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{j=1}^{N_c}
  \mathbb I(T_{\mathrm{solve},j}>\Delta t).
  \label{eq:deadline_miss_ratio}
\end{equation}
Fixed-profile separately reports offline generation and online tracking cost.
Use of SQP-RTI or acados alone is not evidence of real-time feasibility. Only
plan differences that survive the shared layer and appear in odometry can be
connected to the executed-motion interpretation in RQ1.

\subsection{RQ4: Path-Feature Selectivity and Limited Configuration Transfer}

\subsubsection{Low-risk path selectivity}

Stage~II-A compares Smooth-only, Fixed-profile, and \SMPCC{} on $C_1+L_1$.
The analysis asks whether the local timing intervention observed on $H_1$
decreases with path risk and whether \SMPCC{} avoids unnecessary completion-time,
tracking, success, or intervention costs. Blocks from $H_1$ and $L_1$ are not
paired across dates; the path comparison is stratified or uses a preregistered
effect-difference analysis.

Small differences on $L_1$ support selectivity only under frozen equivalence or
noninferiority margins for completion time, tracking, success, and intervention,
together with the declared $H_1\!\rightarrow L_1$ effect-difference rule.
Failure to reject a conventional null is not evidence of equivalence.

\subsubsection{Conditional second-container transfer}

Stage~II-B is executed only if the second-container objective, $C_2$, its random
table, and all criteria were frozen before Stage~I. \SMPCC{} updates only the
declared physical quantities such as $\omega_{1,c}$, $c_{h,c}$,
$\eta_{\mathrm{ref},c}$, and $\dot\eta_{\mathrm{ref},c}$; Fixed-profile is
regenerated from the same $C_2$ parameters, while weights, $\zeta$, horizon,
solver, path, motion limits, shared execution layer, and measurement chain remain
fixed.

Results are compared within each container and reported in physical and
freeboard-normalized units. A limited-transfer statement requires the
preregistered within-$C_2$ visual-effect, task-cost, success, and primary
comparator criteria to pass. A nonsignificant container interaction does not
establish equivalence. Parameter-switch or correct/incorrect-parameter replay
supports optimizer sensitivity only; without a separate physical mismatch
protocol it cannot establish the physical necessity of parameter updating.

\subsection{Supporting Measurement and Model Diagnostics}

Visual measurement admission is required before formal acquisition, including
calibration repeatability, missing-frame behavior, synchronization, and
camera-to-mode bandwidth. Modal--vision consistency is a separate supporting
diagnostic and cannot replace the RGB physical comparison or the constructed
phase/replay evidence.

Under the proposed matrix, the \SMPCC{} population available for the supporting
K6-style modal--vision audit grows from 8 planned units after Stage~I to 16 after
Stage~II-A and 24 after conditional Stage~II-B. The audit uses one frozen
analysis script, synchronization rule, lag policy, and failure denominator. It
compares nonnegative amplitude envelopes and therefore does not validate signed
phase or rotation consistency.

Long-horizon propagation and command-regularity studies are optional exploratory
diagnostics. They have no reserved formal sample count, do not create an
additional research question, and do not change the 40/64/88 packages. They
enter an archived or venue-permitted supplement only if an independent
exploratory protocol is executed and the result materially clarifies a boundary
of the main experiment.
