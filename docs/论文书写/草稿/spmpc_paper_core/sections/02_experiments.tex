\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

The evidence is organized in two layers that must not be conflated.  Layer~S
is a frozen, controller-blind independent-plant simulation campaign over
C0--C4 and an input-shaping reference.  Layer~H is a later Scout hardware
campaign with independently calibrated RGB liquid measurement.  The most
recent pre-revision Layer~S freeze passed an asset and readiness audit but has
not been executed; the source changes documented here require a new freeze and
audit.  Layer~H has not started.  Consequently, this section defines estimands
and release criteria but reports no positive anti-slosh performance claim.

The questions follow the dependency order in
\cref{tab:research_questions}.  Source-level correctness and simulation
efficacy are evaluated before any hardware claim, and internal modeled liquid
states never substitute for an independent evaluation signal.

All trials use a path frozen before motion in a static, obstacle-clear
workspace.  No condition invokes online obstacle detection or replanning.
Independent plant liquid truth is available only to the Layer~S evaluator,
not to the controller.  On hardware, RGB is the primary liquid measurement;
the controller's low-order liquid state and any online monitor are mechanism
signals only.

\subsection{Frozen Assets, Data Separation, and Trial Unit}

\subsubsection{Most recent pre-revision formal-simulation freeze}

The most recent completed V3 simulation freeze is bound to Git commit
\texttt{7775dd68}.  Its session manifest freezes the executable and compiler
identity, solver and execution contracts, P2 path and hash, parameterised
offline plan, V3 phase-rejoin artifact, recovery fit and held-out records,
six condition files, independent-plant runner, analysis script, sixteen
formal seeds, and the exact 96-trial order.  The corresponding readiness
report has status \texttt{READY\_NOT\_EXECUTED}, an empty reason list, and
explicitly records that formal trials have not started.  This means only that
the immutable
asset chain for that commit is internally consistent.  The formal runner additionally
requires a human-authored approval bound to the session hash; that approval
has not been issued and none of the 96 trials has run.  Because the source
changes in this revision are not part of commit \texttt{7775dd68}, that
readiness report does not qualify the revised implementation; a new clean
commit, build, session, readiness report, and human approval are required.

The freeze is explicitly simulation-only: it is not a formal robot release,
does not authorize real-robot enforcement, and does not establish physical
parameter validity.  A source or asset change creates a new freeze and
readiness report rather than reusing the current status.

\subsubsection{Separated data pools}

The evidence pools are separated by complete simulation trial or complete
physical trial, never by adjacent samples or video frames:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  &D_{\mathrm{id,fit}},\ D_{\mathrm{id,test}},\quad
  D_{\mathrm{gate,fit}},\ D_{\mathrm{gate,tune}},\
  D_{\mathrm{gate,held}},\\
  &D_{\mathrm{dev,tune}},\ D_{\mathrm{dev,pilot}},\quad
  D_{\mathrm{sim,confirm}},\ D_{\mathrm{hw,confirm}}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:data_pools}
\end{equation}
Identification test data do not select the execution model.  Gate fit data
estimate the base radii and bounds, gate tune data select only one global
shrinkage, and the held-out gate pool is opened once for evaluation and changes
neither the gate nor feedback gains.  Any retuning after a pilot requires a new
pilot and release.  One complete run is the statistical unit.
Controller cycles, path samples, plant integration steps, and video frames are
repeated measurements within that run.  Solver failures, zero requests,
timeouts, and safety interventions remain in the denominator.  Only a frozen
infrastructure-failure rule may replace a run, and the original attempt
remains in the audit log.

\subsubsection{Primary simulation outcome and decision rule}

For seed $b$ and condition $c$, the Layer~S primary outcome is the trial-level
95th percentile of independent-plant liquid height over motion plus the fixed
4-s tail,
\begin{equation}
  Y^{\mathrm{sim}}_{b,c}
  =\operatorname{P95}_{t\in\mathcal W_{\mathrm{motion+4s}}}
  h^{\mathrm{plant}}_{b,c}(t).
  \label{eq:primary_sim_outcome}
\end{equation}
The controller cannot read $h^{\mathrm{plant}}$.  Completion time, path-
tracking 95th percentile, success/failure, zero requests, recovery use,
solver integrity, and command rewrites are retained alongside the liquid
metric.

For comparator $c\in\{\mathrm{C0},\mathrm{C1},\mathrm{C3}\}$, the paired
effect is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_b^{\mathrm{C4}-c}
  =Y^{\mathrm{sim}}_{b,\mathrm{C4}}-Y^{\mathrm{sim}}_{b,c},
  \label{eq:primary_paired_effect}
\end{equation}
where lower is better.  The frozen analysis reports the paired mean and a
paired-bootstrap 95\% interval using 10,000 replicates.  Its minimum meaningful
difference is 0.5 mm.  A positive independent-simulation claim is authorized
only if the upper interval endpoint for every C4--C0, C4--C1, and C4--C3
liquid-height contrast is at most $-0.5$ mm, while C4 remains within the frozen
10\% completion-time and 0.05-m tracking non-inferiority margins against C0
and C1.  Failed trials remain counted.  Because no formal trial has run, all
effect estimates and decision fields are \Pending{}.

\subsubsection{Gate and recovery diagnostics}

Recovery success is labeled independently of the internal gate and of the
subsequently fitted execution set.  Held-out simulation labels use the
independent plant and complete recovery--tail rollout with the frozen path,
liquid-height, terminal pose, velocity, and settling thresholds.  The 9-D gate
and 14-D execution set are then evaluated jointly; execution-set rejection is
also reported separately so that its contribution remains visible.

\begin{table}[t]
  \centering
  \caption{Held-out empirical-gate outcomes.}
  \label{tab:gate_confusion}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
    \toprule
    Gate decision & Recovery succeeds & Recovery fails \\
    \midrule
    Accept & True accept (TA) & \textbf{False accept (FA)} \\
    Reject & False reject (FR) & True reject (TR) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The frozen gate-selection diagnostic implemented by the fitting tool is the
false-accept rate among independently labeled unrecovered samples,
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{FA}|\neg\mathrm{R}}
  =\frac{\mathrm{FA}}{\mathrm{FA}+\mathrm{TR}}.
  \label{eq:false_accept_unrecovered}
\end{equation}
It is reported with a Wilson interval; the frozen acceptance rule applies to
its Wilson upper bound.  Recovered-sample coverage
$\mathrm{TA}/(\mathrm{TA}+\mathrm{FR})$ is reported with its own Wilson
interval.  A second, differently conditioned diagnostic is the false-safe
fraction among accepted samples,
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{FS}|\mathrm{A}}
  =\frac{\mathrm{FA}}{\mathrm{TA}+\mathrm{FA}},
  \label{eq:conditional_false_safe}
\end{equation}
which is reported with a Wilson interval computed using its own accepted-sample
denominator.  The Wilson interval for
\eqref{eq:false_accept_unrecovered} must not be attached to
\eqref{eq:conditional_false_safe}.  The latter ratio is mathematically
undefined when no sample is accepted; the tool then emits a false
\texttt{defined} flag and null estimate/interval together with the zero
accepted count, rather than favorable evidence.  False rejects,
per-phase coverage, worst phase, rejoining time, execution-set rejection, and
zero requests are also reported.  For the frozen feedback $\kappa_i(e_r)$,
policy proposal, recovery rate transaction, safety evaluation, frozen-priority
arbitration, execution-contract finalization, sink receipt/history, and
conditional phase commit are separate rates.  Fit rollouts never reappear as
held-out observations.

The C4--C3 formal contrast complements the classifier table.  C3 logs the same
empirical metric but does not enforce it, whereas both conditions retain the
same 14-D execution sets and recovery policy.  A C4--C3 difference can
therefore be attributed to empirical-gate enforcement, but it cannot convert
the empirical set into a formal recovery certificate.

\subsubsection{Runtime and command integrity}

Layer~S records solve-time p50/p95/maximum, the fraction exceeding 33.3 ms,
achieved control frequency, Full SQP iterations and KKT residuals, independent
constraint and causal-audit status, stale state, artifact/contract failure,
phase-candidate rejection, supervisor branch, residual saturation, recovery
rate limiting, safety rewrite, and final publication receipt.  Every proposed
first command is traced through \eqref{eq:publication_chain}.  Only the final
$u^{\mathrm{pub}}$ may update $b^v$ and $b^\omega$, and a rewritten command
must not commit the proposed phase.  Development unit tests or microbenchmarks
establish wiring and timing plausibility only; the formal runtime table remains
pending until the 96-trial campaign is executed.

\subsection{Layer H: Planned Scout and Physical-Liquid Validation}

\subsubsection{Hardware release sequence}

Layer~H starts only after Layer~S is analyzed and a separate hardware release
is frozen.  Its first gate re-identifies linear and angular command-to-motion
delay, gain, and actuator dynamics from the final command publication
timestamps and held-out Scout motion.  The current software publication
receipt confirms the ROS transport event; it is not a Scout/CAN actuator
acknowledgement.  That distinction must be closed by the hardware logging
contract or covered as identified command-to-motion uncertainty before any
physical enforcement claim.

The next gates freeze source-time alignment among final command, Scout
feedback, odometry, Nokov or equivalent pose, processed IMU, and RealSense RGB;
validate RGB calibration, valid-frame rate, drift, and minimum detectable
difference; and exercise no-motion then shadow-mode operation.  Shadow mode
must show correct candidate selection, full-horizon execution checks,
residual/recovery/zero branching, safety rewrites, history updates, and phase
commit behavior without allowing the candidate command to drive the base.
Only a new human-approved hardware manifest may enable physical enforcement.

Each physical trial records at least:
\begin{itemize}
  \item solver, supervisor, recovery, safety, and final published commands with
    source and publication timestamps;
  \item Scout feedback, odometry, processed IMU, and source-stamped external
    pose;
  \item independently calibrated RGB left/center/right liquid-height signals
    and per-frame validity;
  \item internal liquid state, selected phase, all 14-D execution-set margins,
    terminal empirical metric, solver audits, and publication outcome.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Physical outcome and fairness constraints}

The planned primary physical outcome is the trial-level 95th percentile of
the independently extracted RGB max-LCR signal over a preregistered
motion-plus-tail window,
\begin{equation}
  Y_{\mathrm{RGB}}
  =\operatorname{P95}_{t\in\mathcal W_{\mathrm{motion+tail}}}
  \left(\operatorname{max\text{-}LCR}_{\mathrm{RGB}}(t)\right).
  \label{eq:primary_rgb_outcome}
\end{equation}
Motion onset, arrival, tail duration, invalid-frame handling, physical units,
and left/center/right composition must be frozen before confirmation.  Peak,
RMS, tail RMS, and settling time are secondary.  Completion time, path and yaw
tracking, endpoint error, success/timeout, zero requests, safety events, and
motion/command envelopes are simultaneous fairness outcomes.

The hardware sample size, randomized block order, smallest effect of interest,
and non-inferiority margins remain \Pending{} until RGB detectability and an
independent pilot are available.  Simulation effect sizes are not substituted
for physical variance.  A physical superiority claim requires an RGB benefit
that clears the preregistered effect threshold while task performance,
failures, and safety satisfy their margins.  An improvement obtained only by
becoming materially slower, less accurate, or less reliable is reported as a
tradeoff.

\subsection{Limitations of the Available Evidence}

The current source supports an implemented-method claim, while the historical
\texttt{READY\_NOT\_EXECUTED} evidence applies only to the pre-revision
\texttt{7775dd68} freeze.  Neither supports a formal independent-plant effect,
because the 96 trials and their analysis do not yet exist.  They support no
real-robot efficacy claim, because
hardware G0 identification, shadow operation, RGB/Nokov/IMU calibration, and
physical confirmation have not started.

Even after positive evaluation, interpretation remains bounded by the frozen
path, static obstacle-clear workspace, contracted container/fill and
low-order liquid-model range, identified execution dynamics, and empirical
gate coverage.  The offline plan is a low-dimensional path-speed-profile
search, the online controller is residual trajectory-tracking MPC rather than
MPCC, and $\kappa_i(e_r)$ is a frozen bounded empirical recovery policy rather
than a certified funnel.  No recursive-feasibility, robust-recovery,
collision-avoidance, or spill-free guarantee is made.
