\section{Problem Formulation and \SPMPC{} Method}

\subsection{Problem Scope and Receding-Horizon Framework}

This paper considers a standard wheeled mobile base carrying an open liquid container along a given geometrically feasible reference path. An upstream module provides the path. At each control cycle, the planner receives the current base state, path progress, previous control input, and a liquid modal state propagated by a low-order model. A finite-horizon optimization then selects the base motion and path-progress speed. Only the first action is executed before the problem is solved again from the updated state.

The present formulation addresses online path-progress motion generation near a prescribed path. Its main OCP does not include dynamic-obstacle prediction, occupancy-grid constraints, or collision corridors. Accordingly, the method is not presented as a complete planner for navigation among dynamic obstacles.

Liquid motion enters the prediction layer. It is not appended after optimization as a diagnostic signal. Candidate motions with similar geometric errors can therefore be evaluated differently when they interact with different liquid phases. \Cref{fig:method_structure} summarizes the resulting architecture.

\subsection{Robot-Liquid Augmented Prediction Model}

The robot and path-progress state is
\begin{equation}
  \xr =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    p_x & p_y & \theta & v & s & \omega
  \end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\end{equation}
where $(p_x,p_y)$ is the base position, $\theta$ is its heading, $v$ and $\omega$ are the linear and angular velocities, and $s$ is the reference-path progress. The control input is
\begin{equation}
  \vu =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    a & \alpha & v_s
  \end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\end{equation}
with linear acceleration $a$, angular acceleration $\alpha$, and virtual progress speed $v_s$. The continuous-time base model is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \dot{p}_x &= v\cos\theta, &
  \dot{p}_y &= v\sin\theta, &
  \dot{\theta} &= \omega, \\
  \dot{v} &= a, &
  \dot{s} &= v_s, &
  \dot{\omega} &= \alpha .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:robot_alpha_dynamics}
\end{equation}

Using $\omega$ as a state and $\alpha$ as a control permits direct angular-acceleration constraints and makes changes in rotational motion explicit in the objective. This alpha-state configuration is an implementation-level modeling choice. The methodological distinction of \SPMPC{} comes from propagating robot motion together with liquid dynamic memory, not from the alpha-state structure itself.

Low-order modal models retain the dominant liquid dynamics with a state dimension suitable for online optimization \cite{A01_Leva2021,A02_Guagliumi2021}. For each container-frame direction $i\in\{x,y\}$, define
\begin{equation}
  x_{s,i}=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \eta_i & \dot{\eta}_i
  \end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\end{equation}
where $\eta_i$ is the dominant modal displacement. Its dynamics are
\begin{equation}
  \ddot{\eta}_i
  +2\zeta_i\omega_i\dot{\eta}_i
  +\omega_i^2\eta_i
  =-\kappa_i a_i,
  \label{eq:slosh_modal_dynamics}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_i$, $\zeta_i$, and $\kappa_i$ denote the equivalent natural frequency, damping ratio, and input gain.

For a container rigidly attached to the base, the forward excitation is the linear acceleration. Turning excitation is approximated by centripetal acceleration:
\begin{equation}
  a_x=a,\qquad a_y\approx v\omega .
  \label{eq:slosh_excitation}
\end{equation}
This approximation is intended for local-planning computation and does not replace a high-fidelity free-surface model. Its useful parameter range must be evaluated through sensitivity analysis and external liquid observations.

Combining both directions gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \xl &=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \eta_x & \dot{\eta}_x & \eta_y & \dot{\eta}_y
  \end{bmatrix}^{\top},\\
  \xaug &=
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \xr^{\top} & \xl^{\top}
  \end{bmatrix}^{\top}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:augmented_state}
\end{equation}
The discretized joint model is written as
\begin{equation}
  \xaug_{k+1}=f_d(\xaug_k,\vu_k).
  \label{eq:augmented_discrete}
\end{equation}

For interpretation of the response predicted inside the planner, a model proxy is defined by
\begin{equation}
  H_{\mathrm{model}}
  =
  c_h\sqrt{\eta_x^2+\eta_y^2}
  +\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{para}}\frac{R_c^2\omega^2}{4g},
  \label{eq:h_model}
\end{equation}
where $c_h$ maps modal displacement to a height scale, $R_c$ is the container radius, and $\mathbb{I}_{\mathrm{para}}$ selects an optional quasi-static turning term. The quantity $H_{\mathrm{model}}$ is used for optimization interpretation and model diagnostics. It is not a liquid-surface measurement.

\paragraph{Prediction implication}
When the objective or constraints depend on the propagated $\xl$, the joint OCP can distinguish candidate motions with comparable geometric tracking errors but different phase-dependent liquid responses. A planner without liquid state cannot express this difference explicitly at the prediction level.

\subsection{Slosh-Aware \MPCC{} Formulation}

Let the reference path be $\vr(s)=[r_x(s),r_y(s)]^{\top}$ with tangent angle $\psi(s)$. Its unit tangent and normal vectors are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \bm{t}(s) &= [\cos\psi(s),\ \sin\psi(s)]^{\top},\\
  \bm{n}(s) &= [-\sin\psi(s),\ \cos\psi(s)]^{\top}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $\bm{e}(s)=[p_x,p_y]^{\top}-\vr(s)$, the contouring and lag errors are
\begin{equation}
  \ec=\bm{n}(s)^{\top}\bm{e}(s),\qquad
  \el=\bm{t}(s)^{\top}\bm{e}(s).
  \label{eq:contour_lag_error}
\end{equation}
The former measures lateral deviation. The latter captures inconsistency between the current position and the path parameter.

Given horizon length $N$ and sampling interval $\Delta t$, each control cycle solves
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \min_{\xaug_{0:N},\vu_{0:N-1}}\quad
  &\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\ell_k+\ell_N\\
  \mathrm{s.t.}\quad
  &\xaug_0=\hat{\xaug}(t),\\
  &\xaug_{k+1}=f_d(\xaug_k,\vu_k),\\
  &\xaug_k\in\mathcal{X},\quad \vu_k\in\mathcal{U},\\
  &k=0,\ldots,N-1 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:spmpc_ocp}
\end{equation}
The stage and terminal costs are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \ell_k={}&
    q_c e_{\mathrm{c},k}^{2}
    +q_l e_{\mathrm{l},k}^{2}
    -q_p v_{s,k}
    +q_v(v_k-v_{\mathrm{ref},k})^2\\
  &+q_{v_s}(v_{s,k}-v_{\mathrm{ref},k})^2
    +\|\vu_k\|_{R}^{2}\\
  &+\|\vu_k-\vu_{k-1}\|_{S}^{2}
    +\|\bm{x}_{\ell,k}\|_{Q_s}^{2},\\
  \ell_N={}&
    q_{c,N}e_{\mathrm{c},N}^{2}
    +q_{l,N}e_{\mathrm{l},N}^{2}
    +\|\bm{x}_{\ell,N}\|_{Q_{s,N}}^{2}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:spmpc_cost_terms}
\end{equation}

The contouring and lag terms maintain path following, while $-q_pv_s$ rewards task progress. Speed-reference terms regulate the travel rate. Control magnitude and variation penalties support base execution. The liquid-state cost becomes phase-aware only when it is coupled with the propagation in \cref{eq:slosh_modal_dynamics} and \cref{eq:augmented_discrete}.

The progress reward is essential. Without it, an optimizer could reduce predicted slosh through prolonged low-speed motion or stopping. Liquid-response results must therefore be reported together with arrival time and path error.

The feasible sets include base-state and control bounds, for example
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
  0\leq v_k\leq v_{\max},\qquad
  |\omega_k|\leq\omega_{\max},\\
  |a_k|\leq a_{\max},\qquad
  |\alpha_k|\leq\alpha_{\max},\qquad
  0\leq v_{s,k}\leq v_{s,\max}.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:spmpc_bounds}
\end{equation}

A soft liquid-state boundary can be included as an extension:
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{x,k}^{2}+\eta_{y,k}^{2}
  \leq \eta_{\max}^{2}+\epsilon_k,\qquad \epsilon_k\geq0.
  \label{eq:slosh_soft_bound}
\end{equation}
Penalizing the slack avoids immediate infeasibility under small model mismatch. This boundary is not a common factor in the current core physical ablation and does not establish spill-free operation.

\subsection{Predictive Slosh-Risk Speed-Reference Shaping}

The basic \SPMPC{} formulation already propagates liquid state inside the OCP. For path segments with elevated predicted risk, an optional upper layer can soften the nominal speed reference before the solve. It changes only the $v_{\mathrm{ref}}$ supplied to \cref{eq:spmpc_cost_terms}; the optimized base command is not overwritten.

Let $\mathcal{B}\subseteq[\beta_{\min},1]$ be a discrete set of scaling factors, with candidate speed $v_{\beta}=\beta v_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{nom}}$. Starting from the current liquid state, a simplified $N_g$-step rollout produces a slosh proxy $H_k(\beta)$. Candidate risk is defined by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{B}&=\{\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_{M-1}\},\\
  R(\beta)&=
  \max_{0\leq k\leq N_g}
  \frac{H_k(\beta)}{H_{\lim}} .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:shaper_risk}
\end{equation}
Here $H_{\lim}$ is a model-risk normalization threshold. The acceptable set and raw scaling factor are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{feas}}
  &=\{\beta\in\mathcal{B}\mid R(\beta)\leq\rho\},\\
  \beta_{\mathrm{raw}}
  &=
  \begin{cases}
    \max\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{feas}},
      &\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{feas}}\neq\emptyset,\\
    \beta_{\min},
      &\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{feas}}=\emptyset .
  \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:shaper_selection}
\end{equation}

An asymmetric rate limiter avoids abrupt reference changes. If $\beta_f^{-}$ is the previous filtered value, then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \underline{\beta}
  &=\max\{\beta_{\min},
    \beta_f^{-}-\dot{\beta}_{\downarrow}\Delta t\},\\
  \overline{\beta}
  &=\min\{1,
    \beta_f^{-}+\dot{\beta}_{\uparrow}\Delta t\},\\
  \beta_f
  &=\operatorname{clip}
    (\beta_{\mathrm{raw}},\underline{\beta},\overline{\beta}),\\
  v_{\mathrm{ref}}^{g}
  &=\beta_f v_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{nom}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:shaper_filter}
\end{equation}

Two limited properties follow directly from the construction. First, $v_{\mathrm{ref}}^{g}$ does not exceed a nonnegative nominal reference. Second, if $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{feas}}$ is nonempty, $\beta_{\mathrm{raw}}$ is the largest discrete candidate that satisfies the model-risk threshold. The rate limiter can still produce $\beta_f>\beta_{\mathrm{raw}}$ in one cycle, and an empty candidate set only causes fallback to $\beta_{\min}$. This module is therefore predictive reference shaping rather than a recursive-feasibility, stability, or spill-prevention guarantee.

\subsection{Online Execution and Ablation Definitions}

The current implementation uses an acados-generated SQP-RTI solver with explicit RK integration for the augmented dynamics. The previous solution and reference-path information provide the warm start. Once the OCP is solved, only the first control is executed:
\begin{equation}
  v_{\mathrm{cmd}}=v_0+a_0^{\star}\Delta t,\qquad
  \omega_{\mathrm{cmd}}=\omega_0+\alpha_0^{\star}\Delta t.
  \label{eq:cmd_generation}
\end{equation}
Uniform base limits are applied before publication. The next cycle reinitializes the OCP using the updated base state and propagated liquid state.

The core ablation has two factors: whether liquid-state propagation and its cost are enabled, and whether the control-smoothing configuration is weak or strong. All variants share the reference path, base constraints, horizon, solver, termination criteria, and initial-state convention. Predictive speed-reference shaping is disabled throughout this matrix.
