\section{Experimental Design and Results}

The experiments address one central question: does explicit propagation of liquid dynamics improve the externally observed liquid response beyond stronger motion smoothing alone? Task completion, path error, and control behavior are evaluated at the same time, preventing persistent deceleration from being reported as anti-slosh performance. Because the current formulation follows a prescribed path, the experimental claims are restricted to that setting.

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocol}

All internal variants share the start and goal, reference path, base-motion bounds, prediction horizon, solver backend, and termination criteria. Predictive speed-reference shaping is disabled in the core factorial ablation, leaving liquid prediction and smoothing strength as the two design factors. External planners use the same task definition, with linear velocity, angular velocity, and acceleration limits matched as closely as their interfaces permit.

Three temporal windows serve different purposes. The main window, from 10\% to 90\% path progress, excludes the dominant influence of startup and terminal detection on steady-motion statistics. The full trial determines task success and arrival time. A fixed post-arrival window is reserved for residual oscillation. The current physical table reports the main-window results, with repeated trials summarized as mean $\pm$ standard deviation.

Real liquid response is evaluated using the calibrated RGB envelope $H_{\mathrm{vis}}$, for which p95 and RMS are the primary metrics. The internal quantity $H_{\mathrm{model}}$ follows the convention in \cref{eq:h_model} and is used only to explain the response predicted by the planner. Matching units do not make the two quantities equivalent.

Model and external liquid quantities are interpreted in separate evidence layers. Agreement in group-level trends can indicate that the low-order prediction is consistent with the external response. Frame-level phase fidelity, reconstruction of true free-surface height, and the validity of a safety threshold require stronger calibration and sensing evidence.

\subsection{Factorial Ablation of Slosh Prediction and Motion Smoothing}

The four variants in \cref{tab:method_variants} form a two-factor matrix. \Bzero{} versus \Bsmooth{} measures smoothing without a liquid model, while \Bzero{} versus \Bslosh{} evaluates the liquid mechanism under weak smoothing. A complete matrix also requires \Bsmooth{} versus \Bours{} to determine whether liquid prediction remains beneficial under strong smoothing. The remaining one-factor comparison, \Bslosh{} versus \Bours{}, characterizes the effect of increased smoothing when the liquid model is active.

\Bsmooth{} versus \Bslosh{} is a diagonal contrast. It has practical narrative value because it tests whether slosh-aware planning with weak smoothing can outperform a strong smooth-only baseline. However, liquid prediction and smoothing strength change together, so the contrast cannot be interpreted as the pure main effect of liquid prediction.

The current physical statistics cover \Bzero{}, \Bsmooth{}, and \Bslosh{}. Without \Bours{}, the matrix cannot estimate the liquid-prediction effect under strong smoothing or identify an interaction between the two factors. The available data therefore provide trend-level evidence rather than a complete causal ablation.

\subsection{Model Sensitivity and Online Computation}

The suitability of a low-order model as an online decision kernel cannot be assessed from nominal-model proxies alone. A sensitivity study should perturb natural frequency, damping ratio, and input gain while also changing the initial liquid state. Natural frequency can be scanned at $\pm10\%$ and $\pm20\%$ around its nominal value, with a wider relative range for damping. Nonzero initial modes represent residual oscillation that has not decayed before the next turn. Relevant outputs include an external or high-fidelity liquid metric, task completion, and degradation of $H_{\mathrm{model}}$ relative to the evaluation signal.

Online computation is characterized by a distribution rather than a single mean. Median and p95 solve times should be accompanied by maximum latency, deadline-miss rate, solver failures, and the achieved control frequency. The use of acados SQP-RTI establishes the implementation route. It does not replace timing measurements on the deployed system.

Until those statistics are complete, no broader claim is made about parameter robustness or real-time performance beyond the observed physical operation.

\subsection{Comparison with Ordinary Online Motion Planners}

The internal \Bzero{} variant is the primary ordinary \MPCC{} baseline for identifying the effect of liquid prediction because it shares the path-progress state, constraints, horizon, and numerical framework with \SPMPC{}. The main external comparison should retain one reproducible ordinary MPC or MPCC local planner and LT-DWA as a stronger long-horizon kinodynamic baseline \cite{D02_Jian2023}. DWA and TEB can provide supplementary classical references \cite{D15_Fox1997,D16_Rosmann2017}.

The comparison follows standard local-planner measures of task efficiency, tracking, smoothness, and computation \cite{D21_Wen2021}, while adding the same external liquid observation for every method. External planners do not receive liquid state as feedback. Only \SPMPC{} propagates the low-order liquid dynamics.

In an obstacle-free fixed-path experiment, this comparison reflects online command generation, tracking, and liquid-transport behavior. It does not exercise the main local-obstacle capabilities of DWA, TEB, or LT-DWA and therefore cannot support a ranking of complete navigation performance. No quantitative ordering is stated before matched results are available.

\subsection{Physical Liquid-Response Validation}

The physical validation uses a fixed reference path and the same nominal speed, $v_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.20\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Each of the three variants contains three representative trials. Linear velocity, angular velocity, and acceleration bounds are aligned, and every trial reaches the goal. Both the model proxy and the RGB envelope are evaluated from 10\% to 90\% path progress.

Stronger smoothing does not produce a consistent external liquid benefit in these trials. Relative to \Bzero{}, \Bsmooth{} reduces $|\omega|$ p95 from $0.234$ to $0.202\,\mathrm{rad/s}$, and its model proxy decreases slightly. The RGB p95 and RMS instead increase to $1.272$ and $0.639\,\mathrm{mm}$. A lower high-percentile angular velocity alone is therefore insufficient to establish improved liquid response.

In the diagonal comparison between \Bslosh{} and \Bsmooth{}, model p95 and RMS decrease by $36.4\%$ and $37.4\%$, respectively. RGB p95 decreases by $29.2\%$, while RGB RMS decreases by $15.9\%$. The angular-velocity p95 of \Bslosh{} is slightly higher, so the external-liquid difference cannot be attributed solely to lower angular velocity. This is the clearest positive trend in the current evidence.

There is a cost. The mean arrival time of \Bslosh{} is $36.851\,\mathrm{s}$, longer than for the other variants, and its projection-error and angular-velocity statistics are not uniformly best. Relative to \Bzero{}, it lowers RGB p95 while leaving RGB RMS nearly unchanged. The current result thus supports a liquid-response trend on the representative fixed path, not dominance across all task metrics.

The internal proxy and RGB metrics move in the same group-level direction when \Bslosh{} is compared with \Bsmooth{}. This consistency helps explain why the low-order model changes the selected motion. It does not establish frame-level fidelity between the two time series, and $H_{\mathrm{model}}$ cannot replace external liquid observation.

These results come from a small representative sample and do not form a complete four-cell physical matrix. Matched \Bours{} statistics, model sensitivity, solve-time distributions, and ordinary-planner results are still absent. Consequently, the current evidence does not support claims of statistical significance, parameter robustness, or broad planner superiority.
