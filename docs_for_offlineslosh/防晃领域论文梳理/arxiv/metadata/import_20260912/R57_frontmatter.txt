                                                   Abstract
arXiv:2212.12246v1 [physics.flu-dyn] 23 Dec 2022




                                                       Nonisothermal liquid sloshing in partially filled reservoirs can significantly enhance heat and mass transfer between liquid
                                                   and ullage gasses. This can result in large temperature and pressure fluctuations, producing thrust oscillations in spacecraft and
                                                   challenging thermal management control systems. This work presents an experimental characterization of the thermodynamic evo-
                                                   lution of a cylindrical reservoir undergoing sloshing-induced thermal de-stratification. We use a 0D model-based inverse method to
                                                   retrieve the heat and mass transfer coefficients in planar and swirl sloshing conditions from the temperature and pressure measure-
                                                   ments in the liquid and the ullage gas. The experiments were carried out in the SHAKESPEARE shaking table of the von Karman
                                                   Institute in a cuboid quartz cell with a cylindrical cut-out of 80 mm diameter in the centre, filled up to 60mm with the cryogenic
                                                   replacement fluid HFE-7200. A thermal stratification with ∆T “ 25 K difference between the ullage gas and liquid was set as
                                                   the initial conditions. A pressure drop of 90% in the ullage gas was documented in swirling conditions. Despite its simplicity, the
                                                   model could predict the system’s thermodynamic evolution once the proper transfer coefficients were derived.
                                                   Keywords: Sloshing, non-isothermal, pressure drop, thermal destratification, 0D model, inverse method, data assimilation
